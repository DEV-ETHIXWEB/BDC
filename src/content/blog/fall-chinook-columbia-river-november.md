The question Captain Clinton hears most in November is whether the Chinook run is still going. The honest answer is that the run is winding down, and the rules have already changed. Knowing that before you book makes for a much better day.

## What you can keep on the Columbia in November

Fall Chinook fishing on the lower Columbia is strongest from August through October. In 2026, ODFW's rules for the stretch from Buoy 10 up to Bonneville Dam have Chinook retention ending well before November, and **all Chinook must be released** from there on.

What is open:

- **Hatchery coho**, from November 1 through December 31.
- **Hatchery steelhead**, from November 1 through December 31.
- **A daily limit of two hatchery salmon or steelhead combined**, coho and steelhead only.

Wild coho must be released, and the steelhead you keep must be hatchery fish. Rules can change during the season, so check [ODFW's Columbia River regulations](https://myodfw.com/news/columbia-river-2026-summerfall-salmon-and-steelhead-seasons-announced) before you go.

That makes a November trip on the Columbia a hatchery coho and steelhead trip, with a chance at a Chinook you photograph and release. It is not a trip for a Chinook dinner, and a good guide will tell you so before you book.

## What fall salmon fishing looks like on the Columbia

The Columbia is Oregon's largest river, and Captain Clinton describes it as big-water Chinook trolling. Trolling herring, spinners or plugs is the go-to way to fish it, and presentation and depth control matter more here than almost anywhere else.

Fall salmon run August through November, with fall Chinook strongest August through October. Spring and early Chinook run March through July, so the river gives you two separate windows each year. For the bigger picture, see our [Columbia River fishing guide](/article/columbia-river-fishing-guide).

Chinook run roughly 10 to 50 pounds and 30 to 59 inches, which is a big part of why even a catch and release fish is worth the trip.

Salmon trips on the Columbia run aboard the 22 foot Willie Predator, which seats up to 6 anglers. Rods, reels, tackle and bait are included.

- **Full day:** 8 hours, $250 per person.
- **Half day:** 4 hours, $150 per person.

Trips can start as early as 4:00 AM for salmon, and a 20 percent tip is customary. Captain Clinton confirms your meeting spot and launch time when you book.

## License and tags for the Columbia

Anyone 12 or older needs an Oregon fishing license. For salmon, steelhead or sturgeon on the Columbia you also need a Combined Angling Tag and a Columbia River Basin Endorsement, since the endorsement covers the Columbia and every river that flows into it. Our [2026 license and cost guide](/article/oregon-fishing-license-guided-trip) has the current prices, and the [river by river breakdown](/article/get-your-valid-oregon-fishing-license) shows where each one applies.

## Another November option: crab

If a coho and steelhead day is not what you had in mind, November also falls inside BDC's Dungeness crab season, October through December. It is a 5 hour trip at $150 per person, and the payoff is crab for dinner. Our [family crab trip guide](/article/family-crab-charter-oregon-what-to-expect) explains how it works.
