Winter steelhead season on Oregon's coastal and tributary rivers runs January through April. Captain Clinton McCulloch guides it from a 16 foot Alumaweld drift boat, and the number of dates on that boat is limited, so it pays to call before the season starts rather than after.

This guide covers the five rivers BDC fishes for winter steelhead, which two groups they fall into, and the license you need for each.

## Five rivers, two different fisheries

Not every river on this list drains to the same place, and that decides your paperwork.

- **Wilson River:** a coastal river that runs to Tillamook Bay. See the [Wilson River guide](/article/wilson-river-fishing-guide).
- **Trask River:** runs into Tillamook Bay as well. See the [Trask River guide](/article/trask-river-fishing-guide).
- **Nestucca River:** empties into Nestucca Bay near Pacific City. See the [Nestucca River guide](/article/nestucca-river-fishing-guide).
- **Sandy River:** a Columbia River Basin tributary that meets the Columbia near Troutdale. See the [Sandy River guide](/article/sandy-river-fishing-guide).
- **Clackamas River:** flows into the Willamette and on to the Columbia. See the [Clackamas River guide](/article/clackamas-river-fishing-guide).

The Wilson, Trask and Nestucca are coastal rivers that run to the Pacific and its bays. The Sandy and Clackamas are Columbia Basin tributaries.

## What license you need, and where it changes

Every one of these rivers needs an Oregon angling license and a Combined Angling Tag, which anyone fishing for salmon or steelhead needs regardless of age.

The difference is the [Columbia River Basin Endorsement](https://myodfw.com/articles/who-needs-columbia-river-basin-endorsement). The Sandy and Clackamas are Columbia Basin tributaries, so both need it on top of the license and tag. The Wilson, Trask and Nestucca are outside the Columbia Basin, so it is not required there.

For 2026, a resident annual angling license is $50, the Combined Angling Tag is $69, and the endorsement is $9.75 when bought with the license. Buy all three through [ODFW](https://myodfw.com/fishing/licensing-info) or a license agent before your trip. Our [2026 license and cost guide](/article/oregon-fishing-license-guided-trip) has the full price list and the nonresident rates.

## A winter steelhead trip with BDC

Steelhead trips run aboard the 16 foot Alumaweld drift boat, which takes up to 3 guests. The guide, boat, rods, reels, tackle and bait are included.

- **Full day:** 8 hours, $250 per person.
- **Half day:** 4 hours, $150 per person.

A 20 percent tip is customary. Captain Clinton has guided these rivers for more than 45 years, and a small boat means one guide reading the water for two or three anglers. See the boat on the [full day](/oregon-fishing-charter-rates/oregon-fishing-charter-full-day-drift-trip) and [half day](/oregon-fishing-charter-rates/oregon-fishing-charter-half-day-drift-boat-trip) trip pages.

## Why call before January

The season starts in January, and drift boat dates are limited across the four months. Calling ahead gives you more choice of dates than waiting until the season has started. Rivers also change with rain, so Captain Clinton picks the river when you book, based on where the fishing is best for your dates, rather than locking one in months ahead.

## Common questions

**When is the best time to fish winter steelhead?** Steelhead fishing is usually best as a river drops and clears after rain, since high, muddy water tends to fish poorly. Your guide decides the day and river with you.

**Do I need my own license for a guided trip?** Yes. Every angler needs their own angling license and Combined Angling Tag, plus the Columbia River Basin Endorsement on the Sandy or Clackamas.

**What should I bring?** Warm, weather-appropriate clothing, your own food and drinks, and your license and tags. Our [what to bring guide](/article/what-to-bring-on-an-oregon-fishing-charter) has the full checklist.
