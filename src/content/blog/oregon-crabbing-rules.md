Crabbing is the easiest way into Oregon's water. No casting, no waiting for a bite that may not come, and most trips end with a cooler of Dungeness crab. The rules are short, but they matter, because a crab that is too small or the wrong sex has to go back every time.

Here is what you need to know, in plain words. Rules can change during the season, so check the [ODFW shellfish rules](https://myodfw.com/articles/oregon-shellfish-regulations) before you go.

## Who needs a shellfish license

Everyone 12 years and older needs an Oregon shellfish license to keep crab. Kids under 12 do not need one, which makes crabbing one of the best family days on the water.

A shellfish license is separate from a fishing license. If you are only crabbing, the shellfish license is all you need. You can buy one online from ODFW, through the MyODFW app, or at any license agent.

You also do not need Oregon's new Ocean Endorsement for crab. ODFW says it is not required if you are only taking shellfish, like Dungeness crab or clams.

## How many crab you can keep

- **Dungeness crab:** 12 per person per day, males only.
- **Red rock crab:** 24 per person per day, any size, either sex.

The daily limit is per person, so a group of four can keep up to 48 Dungeness between them, as long as every crab is a legal male.

## How to tell a keeper Dungeness

A legal Dungeness crab is a male that measures at least 5¾ inches across the shell.

**Measuring.** Measure in a straight line across the back, just in front of the points on each side, not tip to tip. A crab that is close is not good enough: if it is under 5¾ inches, it goes back.

**Male or female.** Flip the crab over. A male has a narrow, pointed flap on its belly. A female has a wide, rounded flap. Females always go back.

On a [BDC crabbing charter](/oregon-fishing-charter-rates/oregon-crabbing-charter-bdc-guide-service) the guide sorts and measures with you, so nobody goes home with a crab they should not have.

## When and where you can crab

- **Bays, beaches, estuaries, tide pools, piers and jetties** are open all year, 24 hours a day.
- **Ocean waters are closed for crab from October 16 to November 30.**

Crabbing can also close for a while because of natural toxins in the water. Before you harvest, check the Oregon Department of Agriculture shellfish safety line at 800-448-2474 or on their website. Your guide keeps an eye on this too.

## Quick checklist

- Shellfish license for everyone 12 and older
- Keep males only, 5¾ inches or more across the shell
- Up to 12 Dungeness and 24 red rock crab per person per day
- Ocean closed October 16 to November 30, bays open all year
- Check the shellfish safety line before you harvest
